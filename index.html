Hively Model (EED + Patent)

Uses the more complete electrodynamics (EED) framework. A monopole with skirt balun launches a pure gradient-driven current, producing an isotropic SLW with B ≈ 0.

P_rad = I² / (4π) × Z₀    S(r) = P_rad / (4π r²)

Detector: palm-sized bifilar coil (face-on, 78.5 cm²). No skin effect. Output current derived from power delivered to 50 Ω load with 50% overall efficiency.

Reference: Hively & Land (2021) J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 1956 012011 + US 9,306,527 B1

Nikolova-Zimmerman Model (2007)

Based on 2007 McMaster University experiments detecting time-dependent electromagnetic potentials at 1.3 GHz using a folded dipole transmitter and biased plasma (fluorescent tube) detector.

A_m = μ₀ I_m / (2π k₀ r)    I_RMS = R · (A_m / √2)

The longitudinal vector potential falls as 1/r. Received power is generated locally in the biased plasma detector (not extracted from the propagating wave energy). This leads to different scaling with transmitter current and range.

Reference: Nikolova & Zimmerman, CEM-R-46, McMaster University, Nov 2007

Educational Comparison Tool

This combined simulator merges two independent SLW models for side-by-side evaluation at identical range. The Hively model follows classical radiated power scaling (1/r²). The Nikolova-Zimmerman model uses measured potential amplitude scaling (1/r) with a plasma-based detector whose output power is supplied locally. Both are educational illustrations only. Real-world validation, efficiency, and system losses are not included.
